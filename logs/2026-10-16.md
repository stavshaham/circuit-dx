## Parts used

| Position | Part | Label |
|---|---|---|
| R_p (series protection) | 1 kΩ | R1K-c |
| R1 | 10 kΩ | R10K-a |
| C1 | 100 nF (104) | C104-a |
| R2 | 10 kΩ | R10K-e |
| C2 | 100 nF (104) | C104-b |

Topology: input → R_p → R1 → node V1 (C1 to ground) → R2 → output (C2 to ground)

## Results / observations

| Test | Probe point | Measured | Expected | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Input | ESP32 3V3 (V_in) | 3.27 V | ≈ 3.3 V | ✅ |
| 2. Healthy | V1 | 3.27 V | = V_in | ✅ |
| 2. Healthy | Output | 3.27 V | = V_in | ✅ |
| 3. C2 shorted | Output | 0 V | 0 V | ✅ |
| 3. C2 shorted | V1 | 1.55 V | 3.27 × 10/21 = 1.557 V (nominal) | ✅ (−0.5%) |

- **Test 2:** at DC the capacitors draw no current, so there is no voltage drop across the resistors — V1 and the output equal the input
- **Test 3:** shorting C2 turns R_p + R1 + R2 into a voltage divider; V1 sits across R2 (10 kΩ of 21 kΩ total). The 0.5% difference is within resistor tolerance and multimeter accuracy
- **Conclusion:** every component is in the correct row, the resistor chain and ground connections are correct, and the C2-short fault behaves as predicted

## Surprises and problems

- Confused the Test 3 jumper with the existing C2-to-ground wire. They are different: the existing wire is C2's normal connection to ground; the Test 3 jumper bypasses C2 entirely (row 26 → ground) to simulate a short. The Test 2 result (3.27 V at the output) confirmed the existing wire was not shorting C2
- First photos were not usable as a record (taken at an angle with the keyboard in frame, then too dark with fingers covering the ground rail). Final photo: straight down, nothing blocking the board, row numbers visible
- Several resistor leads are long and cross each other in the air (around rows 22–24 and 30–35). The circuit works, but loose crossing leads risk accidental shorts and shifting measurements when parts are swapped for fault experiments

## Decisions

- Power the circuit from the ESP32's 3V3 pin for DC checks; the multimeter's reading of 3V3 (3.27 V) is used as V_in in calculations
- Keep Circuit 1 assembled for week 3 measurements
- Record photo changes to the physical circuit as separate `hw(circuit1)` commits so the history shows when the hardware changed
