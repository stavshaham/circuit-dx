# 2026-10-17 — Capacitance meter and as-built frequency response

**Date:** 2026-10-17
**Time spent:** ~1 hour
**Phase / step:** Weeks 1–2 — parts measurement (capacitors), theory (as-built curve)

## Goal for this session

- Measure the capacitors used in Circuit 1 (the multimeter has no capacitance mode)
- Compute the frequency response of the circuit as actually built, using measured part values

## What I did

- Wrote a capacitance meter for the Arduino Nano (`firmware/cap_meter/cap_meter.ino`):
  - charges the capacitor through a known series resistance (R10k-b + R10k-c + R10k-d) and times how long it takes to reach one time constant (ADC threshold 647 of 1024, ≈ 63.2%)
  - C = t / (R · −ln(1 − 647/1024)); ratiometric, so independent of the exact USB supply voltage
  - 20 runs averaged per measurement; fast ADC prescaler (~13 µs per reading)
- Re-measured the three series resistors for the meter (R10k-b, R10k-c, R10k-d) → total 29 480 Ω
- Measured C104-a and C104-b (Circuit 1's capacitors) and C104-c
- Extended `sim/reference_curve.py` to compute an **as-built** curve alongside the nominal one:
  - looks up the measured values in `parts.csv` by part label, using a position → part mapping (Rp = R1k-c, R1 = R10k-a, R2 = R10k-e, C1 = C104-a, C2 = C104-b)
  - saves `results/circuit1_asbuilt.csv`; plots nominal (dashed) and as-built (solid) curves with both cutoff markers
  - SciPy cross-check and monotonic-gain assertion applied to both curves

|---|---|---|

- `circuit1_reference.csv` is unchanged (identical to the 2026-10-15 file) — adding the as-built curve did not affect the nominal one
- **The as-built curve is the reference for the first real ESP32 measurement (week 3)**: any remaining difference will then come from the measurement hardware, not from part tolerances

## Surprises and problems

- **Capacitors had not actually been measured on 2026-10-15.** The `0 → OL` entries came from the multimeter's resistance mode (the reading climbs as the capacitor charges) — this shows a capacitor is not shorted but does not give its value. Built the Nano meter instead
- **Confusing early readings (119 nF, then 108 nF).** Changing R in the sketch from 29 640 to 29 480 Ω can only change results by 0.5%, so a 9% drop pointed to something else. Re-measuring a labelled capacitor (C104-a) four times gave a stable 118.0 nF — the meter was fine; the unlabelled readings were simply different capacitors. Lesson: always note the label before pressing Enter
- **R10k-c re-measured 2% lower** than on 2026-10-15 (9 700 vs 9 900 Ω) — larger than normal meter variation. A third careful measurement confirmed **9 700 Ω**, so the 2026-10-15 reading was most likely affected by probe contact or fingers on the leads. `parts.csv` and the meter's `R_OHMS` (29 480 Ω) already use the confirmed value
- **C104-b readings crept upward** (91.5 → 92.5 nF) over four runs — likely the capacitor cooling after being warmed by handling. Fix: hold parts by the legs and wait ~10 s after inserting
- **`parts.csv` notes with commas broke the CSV columns** → replaced with semicolons
- **Script bugs while adding the as-built curve**, all caught before committing:
  1. looked up columns `name`/`value` that do not exist in `parts.csv` (should be `id`/`measured`, plus a position → label mapping)
  2. `f` used inside `get_data()` but never defined → frequencies now passed in as an argument
  3. `int()` on the capacitor values turned 1.18 × 10⁻⁷ F into **0**, which would have made the as-built filter a flat 0 dB line, therefore, used `float()` instead
  4. as-built cutoff marker reused the nominal index and values
  5. y-axis limit (−40 dB) clipped the as-built curve (−40.78 dB) → widened to −45 dB

## Decisions

- Measure capacitance with the Nano RC-timing meter (no capacitance mode on the multimeter)
- Measure only the capacitors needed now (C104-a/b in Circuit 1, C104-c as a spare); measure the rest before the drift-fault experiments (weeks 6–8)
- Keep the nominal and as-built curves in **separate CSV files** (CSV for Git-friendly diffs; no Excel)
- Compare future ESP32 measurements against the **as-built** curve, not the nominal one
- The simulation reads part values from `parts.csv` by label, so re-measuring a part updates the results automatically
